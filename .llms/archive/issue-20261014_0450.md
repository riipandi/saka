# 55 archived handovers predate the frontmatter convention

- status: resolved
- plans: — (found while fixing `handover-20261012_0500.md`, the live
  directory's one frontmatter-less file)

## Finding

The handover frontmatter convention (`captured` / `plan` / `wave` /
`status`) was adopted partway through the project's history: the five
newest handovers and everything after 2026-10-09 carry it; 55 of the 62
files archived to `.llms/archive/` (everything through 2026-10-09) start
directly at the title. The archived bodies are readable — the filename
still carries the date stamp, and the plan reference is usually the
first line of prose — but no tool can join the archive on the metadata
the newer files carry.

## Resolution (2026-10-14)

The trigger fired: the retrofit ran as one pass, every file read rather
than scripted, `captured` derived mechanically from the filename stamp
(`+07:00`), `plan` from the body's first plan reference, `wave` and
`status` from the closing prose — a key the body cannot name is omitted
rather than guessed (no archived handover states a wave number except
the two whose scope line did, and `plan` is omitted where the body names
none). The validation sweep then caught four files whose pre-existing
frontmatter disagreed with the convention and normalized them into it:
`handover-20261002_2118.md` (an older dialect — `date`/`branch`/`topic`
— now `status`/`captured`/`plan`), `handover-20261010_0545.md` and
`handover-20261010_0835.md` (the roadmap-era `status: open`/`created`/
`scope` dialect — `complete` per their closing prose, the scope lines
became `wave: 1` and `wave: 1.5`), and two stamp typos
(`handover-20261011_1750.md` said 17:55, `handover-20261012_0125.md`
said 01:20 — both now match their filenames). The sweep also fixed the
same typo in the live `handover-20261012_2150.md` (21:45 → 21:50). The
index's Handovers section now records the convention as holding over the
whole archive. Vocabularies: `status` is `complete` (the session's work
or plan finished, deferred candidate threads are not leftovers) or
`partial` (the prose explicitly leaves session work open).
