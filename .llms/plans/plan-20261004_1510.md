# Plan — OAuth SSO token retrieval and local offboarding

Written 2026-10-04. This plan ships the two follow-ups the SSO attribute-mapping
round named first: the provider tokens rest sealed on the binding and nothing
reads or refreshes them, and a provider identity that dies at the source
leaves its saka account enabled with its sessions alive. SCIM inbound is
explicitly deferred by the owner — it is a separate surface, not a dependency
of either piece here.

## Objective

- **Token retrieval**: the holder reads the provider tokens of their own
  binding — the access token to call the provider's API on the holder's
  behalf, refreshed against the provider when expired, the rotation stored.
- **Local offboarding**: a recurring job proves each binding's identity
  against its provider through the refresh-token grant; a provider that
  answers `invalid_grant` has withdrawn the identity, and the account is
  offboarded locally — a permanent ban, every session revoked, one audit
  record.

Design-doc row touched: `.llms/auth-design.md` § SSO Connections (the row
gains its tick in the final phase).

## Current state

Every claim verified in the code:

- The binding's tokens rest sealed: `00016_create_oauthsso_tables.sql:55-56`
  (`access_token` / `refresh_token`, `enc:`-checked, empty when the provider
  answered none). There is **no expiry column** — the age of an access token
  is unknown to the row.
- The tokens are written **once, at the first bind**:
  `flow.go:168-191` seals the identity's tokens at the callback,
  `repository.go:324-326` carries them into `CreateLinkedAccount`, called from
  `bindIdentity` (`resolution.go:577-589`) on the link and JIT branches only.
  The binding branch (`resolution.go:258-261` → `openSession:660-682`) applies
  the profile and opens the session and touches no token column.
- The retrieval surface does not exist: `OAuthSSOService`
  (`api/connect/authn.proto:1031-1097`) carries ten procedures, none reads a
  binding's tokens; `ListLinkedConnections` answers metadata only.
- The offboarding hammer exists: `session.Service.RevokeAllForUser`
  (`modules/identity/session/service.go:655`) revokes every live session of an
  account inside a caller's transaction.
- The withdrawal machinery exists: a ban is a row in
  `public.account_restrictions` written through the restrictions service, the
  ban fields answered by the active-ban join the reads carry
  (`.llms/architecture.md:406`), with the `user_banned` audit event
  (`internal/audit/audit.go:260`) and sign-in refusals already wired.
- The job seam exists: `internal/jobs` drives features through lazy seams —
  the `lazyScimSyncer` answers nil without the federation area
  (`.llms/architecture.md:186`) — the shape an `oauth_offboarding` job copies.
- The provider's token endpoint is reachable from this feature: the builtin
  connections carry their endpoints in code, a custom one re-resolves its
  discovery document at flow time (`custom/`), and the connection row stores
  `client_id` and the sealed `client_secret`
  (`00016_create_oauthsso_tables.sql:22-23`).

## Decisions

1. **Offboard action = a permanent ban** (2026-10-04, owner's pick): the
   restrictions feature's row with no expiry, every session revoked through
   `RevokeAllForUser` in the same transaction, and the existing
   `user_banned` audit event carrying the offboarding reason and the
   provider's slug. Rejected: a `disabled`-flag write (a second
   access-withdrawal concept beside the ban, with no audit vocabulary of its
   own); unlink-only (the binding leaves but the dead identity's account
   keeps signing in by whatever other credential it holds); account deletion
   (irreversible, destroys the audit trail for an automated judgment).
2. **The probe is the refresh-token grant** (2026-10-04): the job presents
   the binding's refresh token to the connection's token endpoint with the
   client credentials; `invalid_grant` is the provider's own word for "this
   identity is gone", a success rotates the tokens back onto the binding.
   Rejected: a userinfo check against the stored access token (an expired
   access token is normal after an hour — the probe would offboard every
   holder whose access token aged out); an admin-driven sweep (the hole this
   plan closes is precisely the one nobody is watching).
3. **A binding with no refresh token is uncheckable and skipped** —
   fail-open (2026-10-04). The provider that answered no tokens for the
   scopes asked (`00016:43-44` documents that shape) cannot be judged dead;
   failing closed would offboard those holders on the first pass. The skip
   is logged, not silent.
4. **Retrieval is holder-only, guard `Session`** (2026-10-04, owner's pick):
   the tokens belong to the binding's holder; the procedure refuses a foreign
   binding with the same `not_found` a missing one answers. Rejected: a
   step-up requirement (the read mutates nothing — the refresh it may trigger
   is the binding's own maintenance); an admin variant (no consumer named;
   the compliance story stays with the audit trail).
5. **Refresh on read, with rotation stored** (2026-10-04): the retrieval
   answers a live access token — an expired one (or one inside a short grace
   window) is refreshed first against the token endpoint and the rotation
   persists to the binding, so the next read and the offboarding probe judge
   fresh material. Rejected: answering the stored tokens as they lie (the
   holder would hand a dead token to the provider's API and learn nothing
   from this feature); a background refresher with no reader (work without a
   consumer).
6. **Every sign-in refreshes the binding's tokens too** (2026-10-04): the
   resolution's branches write the flow's fresh tokens onto the existing
   binding inside the transaction they already own, replacing the
   write-once-at-first-bind shape. Rejected: keeping the write-once binding
   (a second sign-in after a provider-side rotation would store nothing new,
   and the offboarding probe would judge month-old material).
7. **No new audit event vocabulary** (2026-10-04): the offboard is a ban and
   records as `user_banned`; the token rotation and the retrieval are reads
   and maintenance, not account facts. Rejected: a dedicated
   `oauthsso_account_offboarded` event (two records for one fact, and the
   webhook catalog would carry the duplicate).
8. **Migration is an in-place edit of `00016`** (2026-10-04, owner's standing
   rule): `oauth_linked_accounts` gains `access_expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ` — the
   instant the stored access token dies, NULL when unknown — and the dev
   database resets during execution.

## Shape and enforcement points

- **Contract** (`api/connect/authn.proto`, OAuth SSO section):
  `GetLinkedAccountTokens(linked_account_id)` answers
  `OAuthLinkedAccountTokens` — `access_token`, `refresh_token`, `expires_at`
  (the access token's death instant), `connection_id`, `provider` — with the
  tokens opened from their seal at the read. Field numbers continue the
  service's messages; the wire names stay snake_case through the shared
  codec.
- **Guard**: `ProcedureRules` names `GetLinkedAccountTokens` `Session`; the
  service refuses a foreign or unknown binding with `not_found`, the same
  answer an existing surface carries.
- **Schema** (`00016_create_oauthsso_tables.sql`, edited in place):
  `oauth_linked_accounts.access_expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ` (NULL when the
  provider answered no expiry), riding the existing sealed-token columns.
- **Token client** (`modules/identity/oauthsso/token_client.go`): one
  refresh-grant caller over the outbound `IdentityFetcher` seam — the token
  endpoint resolved from the connection (builtin in code, custom from its
  stored manual endpoints or a re-resolved discovery document), the client
  credentials opened from their seal, the JSON answer parsed for
  `access_token` / `refresh_token` / `expires_in`. Errors are typed: a
  provider `invalid_grant` is distinguishable from every transport failure,
  because only the first means the identity is dead.
- **Rotation** (`repository.go`): `UpdateBindingTokens` writes the sealed
  pair plus `access_expires_at` in the caller's transaction; the resolution
  branches call it beside `applyResolutionProfile`.
- **Offboarding job** (`internal/jobs/oauth_offboarding_job.go`): one
  recurring hourly queue, the `audit_cleanup` pattern; the processor reaches
  the feature through a lazy seam that answers nil without the identity area.
  The pass walks the bindings that carry a refresh token, bounded in batches,
  probes each through the token client, rotates the survivors, and offboards
  the refused: ban row + `RevokeAllForUser` + `user_banned` in one
  transaction, then the binding row is left in place (the ban, not the
  unlink, is the offboard).
- **Audit**: `user_banned` with the offboarding reason and provider slug in
  the payload; no other event.

## Execution rules

- Atomic commit per phase; each phase lands compiling, linting, and green in
  its own suite. Commit messages:
  1. `feat(oauthsso): linked-account token retrieval`
  2. `feat(oauthsso): refresh and rotate the binding tokens`
  3. `feat(oauthsso): offboard dead provider identities`
  4. `feat(oauthsso): token retrieval and offboarding validation`
- Validation per phase: `go build ./...`, `task lint`, the touched packages'
  tests; the final phase adds `go test -race ./...`, `task rpc:stale`,
  `task db:validate`, and a probe against `build/release/saka`.
- No ALTER migrations: the schema change edits `00016` in place; the dev
  database resets (`task db:reset -- --force --up`, `task db:seed -- --force`)
  during the work — authorized for this plan.
- No backward-compatibility shims: the token columns' semantics change
  in place, nothing reads the old shape anywhere else.
- Docs sync rides the final phase; a behavior that lands mid-plan notes its
  truth in the plan itself, not in half-synced docs.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Token retrieval contract and schema (`feat(oauthsso): linked-account token retrieval`)

- Proto: `GetLinkedAccountTokens` + `OAuthLinkedAccountTokens`; codegen.
- `00016` edited in place: `access_expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ` on
  `oauth_linked_accounts`; dev DB reset and re-seeded.
- Schema, repository, service, handler: the holder reads their binding's
  tokens as stored (opened from the seal), expiry answered when known; a
  foreign or unknown binding answers `not_found`; a binding with no tokens
  answers empty fields, not an error.
- Guard: the procedure named `Session` in `ProcedureRules`; the area's
  forwarding test covers it.
- Yaak: the request row rides the same change; the secrets guard stays green.
- Tests: the retrieval round-trip over the container suite (own binding
  answers, foreign refused, tokenless binding answers empty).

### Phase 2 — Token endpoint client, refresh, rotation (`feat(oauthsso): refresh and rotate the binding tokens`)

- `token_client.go`: the refresh-grant call, typed `invalid_grant`,
  over the fetcher seam; endpoint resolution for builtin and custom
  connections.
- `UpdateBindingTokens` in the repository; the resolution branches write the
  flow's fresh tokens onto the existing binding (decision 6); the first-bind
  write records the provider's `expires_in` as `access_expires_at`.
- Retrieval consults the expiry: an unexpired token answers as stored; an
  expired (or unknown-age) one refreshes first and the rotation persists
  (decision 5); a refresh that fails with anything but `invalid_grant`
  answers the stored tokens with the expiry they have — the read does not
  fail because maintenance did.
- Tests: the fake provider answers `expires_in`, a rotation round-trip, the
  sign-in refresh path, the expired-read refresh path, and the
  non-`invalid_grant` failure keeping the answer.

### Phase 3 — Local offboarding job (`feat(oauthsso): offboard dead provider identities`)

- The `oauth_offboarding` job (hourly, bounded batches) and its lazy seam;
  the processor probes each refresh-tokened binding through the token client.
- Survivors rotate (the same write decision 5 carries); a `invalid_grant`
  answer offboards: the permanent ban row through the restrictions service,
  `RevokeAllForUser`, and `user_banned` in one transaction. Tokenless
  bindings skip with a log line (decision 3).
- Container tests: a dead identity is banned with its sessions revoked and
  the audit record names the provider; a live one rotates and survives; a
  tokenless one is untouched; the pass is bounded.

### Phase 4 — Validation, E2E, docs sync, handover (`feat(oauthsso): token retrieval and offboarding validation`)

- Full suite `-race`, `task lint`, `task rpc:stale`, `task db:validate`.
- E2E (relevant — the mock exists): the ladder gains the retrieval probe —
  a sign-in over the custom sim, then the holder reads their provider tokens
  over the wire — and the offboarding probe: the mock's token endpoint
  refusing the refresh grant (or a tampered refresh token staged through the
  database read), the job run, the account answering banned and its sessions
  gone. Rows removed in the same turn; the server stopped.
- Docs sync: `.llms/architecture.md` (the token client, the rotation, the
  job), `.llms/rules.md` (the retrieval contract and the offboarding rule in
  the OAuth SSO bullet), `.llms/endpoint-reference.md` and
  `docs/api-endpoint.md` (the new procedure), `.llms/auth-design.md` § SSO
  Connections gains and ticks the rows these two features close (status
  marks only), the Yaak collection audited.
- Handover document (`.llms/handover/handover-*.md`): the decisions and their
  rejected alternatives, the fail-open skip, the mock-driven E2E recipe, the
  remaining follow-ups (SCIM inbound stays deferred — owner's word; provider
  logos; SPA screens).

## Explicitly out of scope

- **SCIM inbound** — the owner deferred it: a directory-sync surface writing
  accounts into saka is a separate design (and the `users.custom_attributes`
  ownership rule belongs to it), not a dependency of this plan.
- Provider API proxying — retrieval hands the holder the tokens; saka does
  not become a passthrough client for the provider's API.
- An admin token-read surface — no consumer named (decision 4).
- Proactive background refresh beyond the offboarding pass — rotation
  happens at sign-in and on read; nothing refreshes on a timer for
  freshness alone.
- SAML, enterprise SSO, new builtin providers, provider logos, SPA screens —
  unchanged refusals.
