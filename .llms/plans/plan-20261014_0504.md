# The session rows leave the table once their retention has passed

## Objective

Closes `.llms/issues/issue-20261014_0330.md` (the backend auth audit's open
sweep gap, `.llms/audit/audit-20261014_0329.md`): `public.sessions` is the
one auth table the cleanup machinery never touches, so ended and expired
rows accumulate for the fleet's lifetime. The plan lands a retention sweep
as a named surface — a config key, a recurring job, the indexes the sweep's
predicate needs, and the docs rows they owe.

## Current state (verified in the tree, 2026-10-14)

- The cleanup machinery covers the queue's expired completed rows, the
  unconfirmed TOTP enrollments and pending bridges, the webauthn tokens, the
  signup tokens, and the device-login requests
  (`internal/jobs/cleanup_job.go:88-135`); no session delete exists anywhere.
- The session repository (`modules/identity/session/repository.go`) has no
  delete-by-retention method — its writes are `Create`, `Revoke`, `Rotate`,
  `RevokeLiveForUser`; `FindActiveByTokenHash` is test-only (the audit's
  note, kept).
- `public.sessions` carries `expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL` and
  `revoked_at TIMESTAMPZ NULL` (`internal/database/migrations/00004_create_identity_tables.sql:129-144`).
  Its indexes are `idx_sessions_user_id_expires_at (user_id, expires_at)`
  (line 163 — leads with `user_id`, unusable for a user-less retention
  predicate) and the partial `idx_sessions_rotated_token_hash` (line 148).
  The single-column expiry-index precedent is `idx_auth_tokens_expires_at`
  (line 201), the audit's own comparison.
- The retention precedent is the audit trail: `app.audit_retention_days`
  (`internal/config/types.go:69`, default 90 at `internal/config/defaults.go:244`,
  validated `> 0` at `internal/config/validate.go:272`, published as an
  administrative App row at `internal/config/publish.go:63` and set into the
  published body at `publish.go:366`), carried into the seeder through
  `internal/registry/infrastructure.go:340` and applied by the dedicated
  `audit_cleanup` recurring job (`internal/jobs/audit_cleanup_job.go` —
  daily, `RetentionDays` rides the task payload, the job re-enqueues
  itself). The seeder's pending-count guard (`internal/jobs/register.go:238-260`)
  keeps a restart from multiplying the schedule.
- `entity.TableSessions` exists (`internal/database/entity`, line 41).
- The docs rows the audit precedent owes: the jobs summary in
  `.llms/endpoint-reference.md:367` names the audit retention and its job;
  `docs/configuration.md` documents the config sections.
- No flow reference contradicts the shape: `ListSessions` keeps ended rows
  listed by design (the stamp is the holder's fact); retention deletes rows
  no holder reads anymore — the listing's shape is untouched.

## Decisions

- **D1 (2026-10-14): the sweep is a dedicated `session_cleanup` recurring
  job, not an arm of `cleanupProcessor`.** The horizon is a config-carried
  number of days and the cadence is daily — exactly the `audit_cleanup`
  shape; the cleanup job's arms are fixed one-hour lifetime sweeps whose
  window comment ("the tables' own lifetimes") does not stretch to a
  30-day retention, and threading a second time unit through
  `CleanupTask`'s payload mixes two schedules in one task. This is a
  recorded delta from the issue's remedy text ("a retention sweep in the
  existing cleanup job") — the issue named the mechanism loosely; the audit
  job's existence settles the shape.
- **D2 (2026-10-14): the predicate is `WHERE revoked_at < $cutoff OR
  expires_at < $cutoff`.** Retention counts from the row's end: a revoked
  row ages from its revocation, an expired-never-stamped row from its
  expiry. `revoked_at < $cutoff` is NULL-safe in SQL (NULL compares to
  NULL, the row fails the arm). Rejected: revocation-only (expired
  never-revoked rows accumulate forever — the exact gap the audit found);
  an `expires_at`-only read of "ended" (an early-revoked row would linger
  until its original expiry passed); age-from-`created_at` (a long-lived
  session would be deleted while alive).
- **D3 (2026-10-14): the horizon is `app.session_retention_days`, default
  30, validated `> 0`, published as an administrative App row beside
  `audit_retention_days`.** Sessions are a personal list, not a compliance
  trail — 30 days keeps a holder's recent history readable while the
  lifetime ledger stops growing. Rejected: a runtime-editable settings
  catalog entry (retention is deployment policy, the audit precedent is
  env config, and no runtime-editable retention surface exists yet);
  a duration-formatted key (the config vocabulary for policy windows is
  days — `audit_retention_days`).
- **D4 (2026-10-14): the delete lives in the session repository
  (`DeleteRetentionExpired`), the job is the schedule that calls it.**
  `cleanup_job.go`'s own comment states the rule — "the deletes live in the
  multifactor repository because the tables are its vocabulary; the job is
  the schedule that calls them" — and the sessions table is the session
  module's vocabulary. Rejected: a raw sqlbuilder delete in the job file
  (the audit/webauthn precedent — their tables belong to no feature; the
  sessions table does).
- **D5 (2026-10-14): the sweep's indexes are `idx_sessions_expires_at
  (expires_at)` and `idx_sessions_revoked_at (revoked_at) WHERE
  revoked_at IS NOT NULL`.** The OR predicate is served by a BitmapOr of
  the two; the partial shape matches `idx_sessions_rotated_token_hash`.
  Validated with `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` on seeded rows before and
  after (the datastore rule; skill `postgresql-optimization`).
- **D6 (2026-10-14): no E2E.** The surface adds no route, no endpoint, no
  SPA behavior — `ListSessions`' shape is untouched, and the sweep's
  witness is the container-level job tests plus the release probe.

## Explicitly out of scope

- **No UI and no endpoint.** Retention is deployment policy; the settings
  catalog grows nothing, and no route reads the deleted rows.
- **No runtime-editable retention.** The env-config precedent stands
  (D3); moving retention into the settings catalog is a different surface
  for a different need.
- **`known_devices` is not swept.** Its rows outlive sessions on purpose —
  the first-seen judgement behind the new-device notice reads them
  (migration `00004`'s own comment).
- **No `ListSessions` change.** The listing keeps ended rows until the
  retention deletes them; no pagination, filter, or payload change rides
  this plan.
- **No audit-trail or other-table retention changes.** The audit's 90-day
  window and every other sweep are as they stand.
